I love *Dungeon Crawler Carl* as much as the next guy, so when Matt Dinniman announced this book I was pretty hyped up about it. I listened to the audiobook because Dinniman's DCC series has an insanely terrific track record with the audiobooks, so I simply said "why not?", and off I went.

I went into this book expecting something different from DCC, but I'm not sure it is. I feel like the book is as close to the more popular series as you can get without it actually being part of it. The story centers around Oliver, a colonist of New Sonora, a planet where a portion of Earth's population has migrated. However, capitalist greed and racism towards these New Sonorans gave one of the big monopolies back on Earth, Apex Industries, a great idea: televising a show about AI soldiers remotely controlled by Earth citizens killing the citizens of New Sonora.

Let's start with the similarities: the story's about a normal guy just living his life when suddenly a (technically) alien race threatens to destroy the planet and all its inhabitants. There's sci-fi, comedy, and an anti-capitalist theme throughout. 

That said, there are big differences that set these two series apart. *Operation Bounce House* is very much **not** a LitRPG, this is the big one. Genre-wise, this is more robots, missiles, and internet trolling than pure fantasy elements like magic and enchanted items. Finally, story-wise they manage to be equally different and similar. Both are about outsiders trying to destroy a planet while giving its inhabitants the tiniest of odds of survival. But the difference I found was that DCC has a bigger focus on the "game" itself, the rules, the constraints of what the crawlers can and cannot do, while OBH is more about survival, and not caring about playing "fair" or within the rules (or perceived rules, nothing's really established as far as I can remember).

Make no mistake, it's a good story and I had fun. I just found it difficult to separate it from Dinniman's other work, which keeps me from viewing this story as the standalone it is. It's similar to video game series like *Final Fantasy* (one of my favorites of all time), it's hard to review or rate these games as standalones, which they are. Instead, they are usually compared to other entries in the series. It's a human thing, it's not super logical, but we all do it.

While I wasn't as invested in the story, I still love a good anti-capitalist story, it's the right type of humor for me, and the story itself is well structured and has a solid conclusion. Honestly, if you enjoy *Dungeon Crawler Carl*, odds are you'll at least like this. If you've never read DCC but are interested in some fun sci-fi, you should still definitely check this out. This book is very much on my "to be re-read" shelf, because I feel like I just wasn't in the right mind space my first go around, [which wouldn't be the first time](/reviews/nadie-me-vera-llorar).
