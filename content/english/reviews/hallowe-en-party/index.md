This is the first Agatha Christie book I read, it was chosen for my local book club, I went in with zero expectations and got out feeling like I could do with reading more Agatha Christie books.

This story centers around Hercule Poirot, the protagonist of most of Christie's book, who in this case comes to investigate the murder of a tween girl, Joyce Reynolds, who is drowned in a bucket of water used to bobble for apples. This marks the beginning of this story that includes all the twists and turns you’d expect from a murder mystery. I haven’t read that many books of this genre, so I’ve been making an effort to read more of them this year. In this particular case I feel like the story tried too hard to give/present red herrings to the reader. While I personally didn’t figure out who the murderer was until the very end, I was kind of close, and can actually see how others might have guessed it from pretty early on. I've read other reviews of this specific book and it seems that this might be one of her weaker novels. If that’s the case, then I’m all in on her books, because despite the minor inconvenience I mentioned before, I did like this quite a bit, so if it only gets better from here, sign me up.

The book is also super British. It personally works for me, I'm all for a good bit of sarcastic British humor. As for the story itself, I really liked Christie’s plot work and the mystery in general and how it was handled. I definitely don’t think it was anything outstanding, but for what it is, it is a fun page turner, which is honestly all I look for in these types of books.

I think this is one of those books where even if you’re the slightest bit interested in it, just give it a chance, after a few chapters you’ll either be hooked and keep going, or you won’t and you’ll drop it. You'll either like it or you won’t. As long as you don’t go in expecting the greatest mystery in the history of mankind, but a fun page turner and I think you’ll be fine.
