This is a hard book for me to review. I really liked the last Ojeda book I read [Mandibula](https://www.goodreads.com/book/show/39391224-mand-bula) (Jawbone in English), giving it 4 stars. I had high hopes for *Chamanes* as it's also a horror novel based in South America but the real horror for me was how confusing it was.

Plot-wise, this novel centers around a group of young adults who venture out into a sort of Burning Man-esque music festival in the mountains of Ecuador. On a deeper, thematic level, it's a story about a young woman looking to reconnect with her father, and going to this festival serves two purposes: it's the first step to leaving her hometown and finally taking action to go find her father. It's also a means to escape from the violence that the drug cartels have been enforcing on her hometown, as is the case a lot of the time, these types of festivals, the community, music, and even the drugs, are a means to escape from reality, to forget about the problems back home, and try to forget about them if even for a moment. The narrative bounces between different points of view of a small group of people attending this festival. The coolest narrative device in the whole book I feel is that we get chapters from the points of view of many characters but never from the main character, Noa. This was a really nice touch because her true intentions are always kept a mystery from everyone, and every character has a different perception of her.

I'll be the first to admit I am absolutely not a party person, and I don't do any kind of drugs, I've never even had the smallest of urges to even try a cigarette. So when it became apparent that having a background with these types of substances would've added to my enjoyment of this book, I kind of knew where the rest of my reading experience was headed. One of the highest rated reviews on Goodreads describes this book as the movie "Midsommar", but in Ecuador. I can kind of see the comparison, both are stories revolving around a cult-like festival with a bunch of bizarre things going on. Both have a certain magical realism feel to them. Both have a pretty sad ending.

That's all nice and all, but it just didn't grab my attention all that much. I read this as part of the book club I go to, and honestly if it wasn't because of that, I probably wouldn't have finished it. There are interesting themes and elements to the book. The subplot about the relationship between Noa and her father is interesting. The way the narrative weaves between past and present is also well done, and the prose is great. I just did not connect with all the festival events, the drugs, wondering how much of what is being presented is a hallucination from drugs and how much is real. Either the author also loves these types of festivals or she did a great job with her research because the detail in the sounds, the feelings, even the visions of everything is very detailed.

All in all this book has narrative and literary elements that I liked and found interesting, but at the end of the day, the story itself didn't connect with me. I'm clearly not the target audience for this book, and my opinion is definitely among the minority, most reviews online I've found about this rave about it. I was very confused throughout most of the book, especially towards the end where there's a huge scene with all these magical realism-type things going on, I was just not able to visualize what I was reading. I found the subplot between Noa and her father more interesting than the main plot, and even that didn't have a satisfying conclusion. The theme around cartel violence having such a big, negative effect in the lives of the community is barely touched upon, they mention they're escaping from it, but that's about as far as that topic goes, I think there was a lot of wasted potential with these ideas. I respect the opinions of those that loved this book, but for me, this is the closest to a 1 star book I've gotten since I started this blog.
